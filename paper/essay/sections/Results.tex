\subsection{Genome comparison}

    \label{tab:genome}

$|LCS| = 29\,875$ against lengths 29\,903 and 29\,882 gives a similarity of 0.99906. The LCS counts
the symbols that can be kept in order in both genomes, so $n - |LCS| = 28$ symbols of the Wuhan
genome and $m - |LCS| = 7$ symbols of the Texas genome have no partner. The measure has one
limitation: the LCS ignores locality, and a scattered set of matches scores the same as a contiguous
block of the same size. This is why the gap analysis is needed to say where the differences are.

There are six substitutions: three of them synonymous and three not. The three
non-synonymous ones are D$\to$A in QHD43415\_13 and two in QHD43422, T$\to$I and L$\to$S. The three indels are all after
position 29\,000. One is a 21-nucleotide run of A missing from the Texas record, which reflects how far the sequencing read went, not a mutation. The other two are an insertion
and a deletion three positions apart near 29\,091, and together they are really one more
substitution, C$\to$T at 29\,095: the LCS has no substitution operation, and when the changed base
equals its neighbour (Wuhan \texttt{GCTTTCGG}, Texas \texttt{GCTTTTGG}) an indel pair and a
substitution give the same LCS length, so the traceback may return either. This is the main
limitation of using the LCS as a diff. An edit-distance objective under the same Hirschberg
recursion, which does have a substitution operation, would report the change at 29\,095 as a
substitution. Note that two substitutions also fell outside the three gene files and could only be
classified after backtracking located QHD43422.

\subsection{Search, greedy, backtracking, branch and bound, palindromes}

Pattern search found all three genes at exactly the header coordinates: ORF1ab at 266, S at
21\,563 and M at 26\,523 in 1-based positions. In the Texas genome S and M are found intact while
ORF1ab is not, which is consistent with the substitutions in Table~\ref{tab:mutations}.

Greedy activity selection took 187 candidate ORFs and selected 8, recovering S and M exactly and
ORF1ab as its two large pieces, ORF1a and ORF1b. Lowering the threshold to 30 codons makes greedy
pick 53 small ORFs that cover 30\% of the genome and recover none of the known genes, because the
objective is the count (Table~\ref{tab:orfs}, Figure~\ref{fig:orf}).

Backtracking located 23 of the 24 proteins over their full length, with between 19 and 143 nodes
each. The exception, QHD43415\_11, matches exactly 9 amino acids from position 13\,441, that is, up
to nucleotide 13\,468, the ribosomal frameshift, so the algorithm found the biology rather than
failing (Table~\ref{tab:proteins}).

Branch and bound found, for $k = 8$ and $d = 1$, the motif TGTTGTTA with 75 occurrences, the same
optimum as exhaustive search, using 6\,383 nodes instead of 85\,695 (Table~\ref{tab:motif}).

Manacher gave a longest palindrome of 11, 15 and 20 nucleotides in M, S and ORF1ab
(Table~\ref{tab:palindromes}).

\subsection{Performance}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{fig_time_scaling}
    \caption{Running time against input size (log-log). Dashed lines are the theoretical growth anchored at the largest measurement; slopes in the legends are least-squares fits.}
    \label{fig:time}
\end{figure}

On real DNA the three search methods are all linear, at slopes 1.03, 1.00 and 1.02
(Table~\ref{tab:complexity}). Naive search is not slower than the others because a mismatch ends most
alignments after one or two comparisons. The Z-function is two to three times KMP because it builds
and stores an array of $n + m + 1$ values while KMP keeps one pointer. On the adversarial text naive
search still has slope 1 in $n$, but it is about 20 times slower than KMP, the factor $m = 50$ of the
$O(nm)$ bound; KMP and Z are unchanged. In the LCS panel the pure Python rows and the numpy rows both
have a slope close to 2, quadratic as $O(nm)$ predicts with $n = m$, and Hirschberg has the same slope
and costs about twice the numpy rows, the $2nm$ bound; the pure Python version reaches only
$n = 3\,000$, where one alignment already takes 431.5 ms, while numpy covers the full 29\,903 in
1.68 s. In the palindrome panel centre expansion is linear on DNA (1.04) but quadratic on $A^n$
(2.02), while Manacher is linear on both.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{fig_search_space}
    \caption{Search-space techniques against their baselines: exhaustive subsets versus greedy, encodings enumerated versus backtracking nodes, exhaustive versus branch-and-bound nodes.}
    \label{fig:search_space}
\end{figure}

Exhaustive subset search doubles with every candidate while greedy stays flat. Enumerating
encodings grows by the codon degeneracy at each amino acid and reaches 995\,328 at $k = 10$, while
backtracking stays near 100 nodes, 108 at the same $k$, because after three amino acids the prefix
pins down a single genome position. Branch and bound expands about 7\% of the exhaustive nodes at
$k = 8$, 6\,383 against 85\,695, and keeps growing slowly to $k = 12$, where exhaustive search is no
longer feasible.
