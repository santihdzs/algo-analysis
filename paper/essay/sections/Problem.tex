\section{Problem definition}\label{sec:problem}

\ph{Opening paragraph. Main idea: the assignment asks how different algorithmic strategies can analyse, search, compare and characterise biological sequences. State the decision taken here: rather than one technique, every technique of the course is applied to a concrete question that the problem scenario poses about the SARS-CoV-2 files, and the techniques are then compared on measured cost. The central problem is the comparison of the Wuhan 2019 genome (MN908947.3) with the Texas 2020 genome (MT106054.1): where are they the same, where do they differ, and do the differences change amino acids.}

The mapping between questions and techniques is:
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
    \item Where are the genes M, S and ORF1ab inside the genome? Exact pattern search: brute force as the baseline, then KMP and the Z-function.
    \item How similar are the two genomes and what exactly changed? Longest common subsequence by dynamic programming, made feasible for two full genomes by Hirschberg's divide-and-conquer formulation.
    \item Which open reading frames form the largest non-overlapping set? Greedy activity selection.
    \item Where is each protein of \texttt{seq-proteins.txt} encoded? Backtracking over codon choices with pruning by the genome.
    \item Which motif of length $k$ occurs most often when one mismatch is allowed? Branch and bound over the tree of prefixes.
    \item Which region of each gene is the longest palindrome? Manacher's algorithm against centre expansion (extra technique).
\end{itemize}

\ph{Closing sentence: success is measured by three things per technique: a correct answer checked against an independent computation, a justification of why the technique fits the problem, and a measured running time compared with its theoretical complexity and with a brute-force baseline.}
